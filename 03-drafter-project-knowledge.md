# SGEPT Briefing Style — Essentials

*Upload this file to the Drafter Project's "Project knowledge" tab on `claude.ai`. It is the agent's standing reference.*

## Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

Open with the finding, not the context. A reader who stops after the lead paragraph should know the core argument.

## World Bank topic sentences

Every paragraph's first sentence IS its finding. Subsequent sentences provide evidence for that finding only. A reader who reads only the first sentence of every paragraph should be able to reconstruct the argument. This is the skimmability test.

## Tone — what this voice does

- **Factual.** Every claim backed by specific data. Not "many countries" — "14 of 20 G20 members".
- **Comparative.** Default frame is across jurisdictions, time periods, or instruments.
- **Direct.** State conclusions; do not bury them in qualifications.
- **Precise.** Use the specific term. Not "trade barriers" when you mean "tariff-rate quotas". Not "recently" when you mean "in January 2026".
- **Measured.** Confident where evidence is strong; explicit about uncertainty where it is not. No hyperbole either way.

## Tone — what this voice does NOT do

- No hedging without warrant ("this could potentially suggest")
- No hyperbole ("unprecedented wave of...")
- No advocacy dressed as analysis ("governments must reverse course")
- No vague quantification ("many countries", "a significant number")
- No throat-clearing ("it is important to note that")
- No burying the lead

## Institutional voice

Use: "the data shows", "our analysis indicates", "the evidence suggests". Avoid: "I think", "in my view", "it seems to me".

## Comparative framing — pick one

- Cross-jurisdiction (US vs EU vs China)
- Cross-temporal (2024 vs 2026)
- Cross-instrument (export controls vs investment screens vs tariffs)
- Stated vs revealed (declared objective vs actual product scope)
